About Aurelle

The studio behind the look

Fourteen years, one rented chair turned twelve-artist studio, and a method that has not changed since 2011: your skin first, the trend second.

How it started

One chair, one conviction

Nadia Sethi opened Aurelle in 2011 with a single rented chair in a shared salon on Fillmore Street and a conviction that would not leave her alone: bridal makeup had become too heavy, too uniform, and too far from the person wearing it.

She had spent four years doing runway and editorial work before her first bridal booking, and what struck her was how differently brides were treated — flattened under the same contoured, cameraready mask regardless of their skin, their features or the light they would actually stand in. Aurelle started as a quiet correction to that: fewer products, better matched, applied by someone who stayed in the room until the ceremony ended.

Word moved the way it does in this business — one satisfied bride's photographer, one wedding planner willing to take a chance on a new name. By 2014 the single chair had become a two-artist studio. By 2018, Aurelle was packing kits for destination weddings. The chair is gone, but the conviction that started it has not moved an inch.

What guides us

Four rules we have never broken

Not a brand mission statement — the four working rules that decide what happens in the chair.

Skin first, trend second

We diagnose skin before we discuss looks. A trend that fights your skin never survives fourteen hours of wear.

One artist, start to finish

The person who runs your trial is the person who arrives on your wedding morning. No hand-offs, no surprises.

Tested in your real light

Every trial is photographed in daylight and tungsten, because your reception hall lights nothing like our studio window.

Priced in writing

Your quote, timeline and artist assignment arrive in writing two weeks out — nothing settled by handshake on the day.

What we hold ourselves to
